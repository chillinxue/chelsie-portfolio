Ascenzi & Associates — 0→1 B2B Website

Building a digital product from research to launch for international companies entering the U.S. advanced manufacturing market.

Role
Product Manager & UX Designer
Scope
Product Strategy, UX Research, PRD, Information Architecture, UX/UI Design, Web Development
Industry
Advanced Manufacturing, Semiconductor Construction, B2B Consulting

01

Background — Turning a complex consulting business into a clear digital product

Ascenzi & Associates helps international manufacturers, contractors, and suppliers establish and grow their operations in the United States, particularly within semiconductor and advanced manufacturing construction. The company was building its digital presence from the ground up, and I joined to lead the website from 0 to 1 — starting with understanding the business, its customers, and how overseas companies navigate the complex process of entering the U.S. market.

Rather than treating the website as a marketing page, I approached it as a product:

How can we turn a complex consulting service into a digital experience that builds trust, explains the U.S. market-entry journey, and converts the right visitors into qualified leads?

The product goals were therefore both credibility and lead generation, particularly among semiconductor owners, EPC/GC executives, and international suppliers.

02

The problem — Different customers arrive with completely different problems

An overseas manufacturer entering the U.S. may need help understanding market entry. A contractor may need licensing. Another company may already be operating but struggle with talent acquisition. Through business research and stakeholder discussions, I identified three major audiences:

Semiconductor owners & EPC/GC executives

Need to quickly determine whether the company has credible U.S. construction expertise.

International vendors & suppliers

Need help navigating market entry, regulation, and local business ecosystems.

Internal teams

Need an efficient way to publish industry insights and manage incoming inquiries.

Where am I in my U.S. journey, and what do I need next?

03

My role

I worked across product strategy, UX, and development — conducting market and user research, identifying customer segments and pain points, working directly with leadership to define business requirements, writing the PRD, designing the site's information architecture, mapping service and conversion journeys, defining lead-generation workflows, and coordinating design and engineering through launch.

Research→Requirements→UX→Development→Launch

04

Product strategy — Organizing around customer problems

Research showed users weren't simply looking for "consulting" — they were trying to solve specific operational problems. I structured the product around four service areas that became the foundation of the site's information architecture:

Talent acquisition

Helping companies build qualified U.S. teams.

Licensing

Helping businesses navigate contractor and professional licensing.

U.S. market entry

Connecting the steps required to establish and operate a U.S. business.

Supporting & growth services

Workforce development, facilities, partnerships and advisory services after launch.

05

Designing the conversion journey — From "what does this company do?" to "let's talk"

Because this is a B2B product, one key decision was making conversion part of the product architecture rather than a generic Contact page bolted on at the end. I designed multiple paths toward a conversation:

Service→Understand solution→Contact
Industry insight→Establish expertise→Contact
Homepage→Identify need→Explore service→Contact

The contact experience also begins by asking what type of organization the visitor represents before collecting contact details, so leads carry more useful business context — making the site more than a brochure: the beginning of the customer qualification process.

06

Writing the PRD

This project also gave me the opportunity to translate qualitative business requirements into technical product specifications, covering:

Information architecture

Navigation, services, insights, company information, and contact flows.

Content system

Industry insights with search, filtering, and article categorization.

Internationalization

Multilingual architecture for English and Traditional Chinese, extensible to more languages.

Lead management

Contact forms designed to connect with CRM workflows.

CMS & governance

Editorial permissions and approval workflows for technical content.

The PRD also considers future CRM integration, CMS permissions, and a potential client portal for project and service-status tracking.

Reflection — A website can be a product when it solves a business workflow

The hardest part wasn't designing pages — it was understanding an unfamiliar, highly specialized industry well enough to determine what information mattered, to whom, and at what point in their decision journey.

Start with the decision the user is trying to make, then design the product around making that decision easier.

Designing AI Support Through User Research

Understanding how international graduate students think about mental health, trust, privacy, and AI-powered support.

Role
UX Researcher
Scope
User Interviews, Survey, Contextual Inquiry, Competitive Analysis, Affinity Mapping, Personas, Journey Mapping
+ add research artifacts (affinity map, personas)

01

Background

International graduate students often navigate academic pressure, cultural adjustment, social isolation, and unfamiliar support systems at the same time. Our research explored how technology — and potentially AI — could better support their mental well-being. Instead of starting with an AI solution, we started with the users:

What kind of support do international students actually need, and where should AI fit into that experience?

02

Research approach

I used a mixed-method approach to understand both behavior and underlying motivation:

Surveys

Captured broader patterns around mental health challenges, coping behaviors, and support preferences.

User interviews

Explored personal experiences, expectations, and concerns around AI-driven support.

Contextual inquiry

Examined how students manage stress and seek support in real-world environments.

Competitive analysis

Analyzed existing mental health products to identify gaps and opportunities.

03

Turning research into product insights

I synthesized findings using affinity mapping to group observations into recurring themes, personas to translate research into representative needs and frustrations, journey mapping to trace how students move from stress to recognizing a problem to seeking (or not seeking) support, and problem statements that converted patterns into product opportunities around privacy, accessibility, cultural relevance, and emotional connection.

04

Key product insight

One of the most important findings was that AI availability alone does not create trust. Users also cared about privacy and control over sensitive information, accuracy and relevance of personalized support, cultural sensitivity, clear boundaries between AI and human support, and the ability to access help without feeling judged.

This shifted the product question from “how can we build an AI mental health app?” to:

Where can AI provide meaningful support without reducing trust?

Reflection — AI capability is not the same as user value

Before deciding what AI should generate or automate, product teams need to understand when users want AI involved, what they expect from it, and what would cause them to stop trusting it. That mindset now shapes how I think about AI quality — not only whether an answer is technically correct, but whether it is relevant, trustworthy, contextual, and appropriate for the user's needs.
